My engineering journey is built around self-directed, first-principles learning. As a BCA student at Michael Madhusudan Memorial College, affiliated with Kazi Nazrul University, I use my academic foundation as a starting point while exploring beyond the curriculum through independent projects, experimentation, and deeper technical study.

I am particularly interested in understanding what happens beneath the abstractions we use every day — how processes interact with an operating system, how memory is managed, how applications communicate through network stacks, and how software behaves when it meets the underlying system. I also enjoy exploring Linux as a platform for understanding and experimenting with these concepts.

My work primarily revolves around Python, backend development, command-line tools, and problem solving. I enjoy turning ideas into practical software while gradually working my way down the stack to understand the systems that make it possible. My college studies provide the theoretical foundation, while personal projects and experimentation give me the freedom to explore those concepts beyond the classroom.

I think of this progression through the metaphor of space exploration: starting with what is known, venturing into unfamiliar territory, understanding the environment, and building reliable tools to go further. Every project is another mission — an opportunity to learn, experiment, and map a little more of the technical landscape.
